# PRESENTATION OUTLINE: NeuroScan AI (Deep Learning Focus)

## Slide 1: Title Slide
- Project Name: NeuroScan AI
- Subtitle: Advanced Brain MRI Classification via DenseNet-121 & Grad-CAM
- Team & DL Roles:
    1. Nrusimha Sai (Dataset & Logic)
    2. Srinivas (Architecture & Training)
    3. Durga (Preprocessing & Augmentation)
    4. Roshini (Interpretability & Demo)

## Slide 2: The Problem (Clinical Context)
- Need for automated multi-class MRI classification.
- Targeting: Glioma, Meningioma, Pituitary, and No-Tumor.
- Bridging the Gap: Moving from simple labels to "Explainable AI."

## Slide 3: Dataset Summary
- Total Images: ~3000+ MRI Scans.
- Format: High-res JPEG/PNG standard.
- Balanced across 4 classes for unbiased training.

## Slide 4: Deep Learning Architecture
- Backbone: **DenseNet-121**.
- Why DenseNet? Feature reuse, efficient parameter usage, and vanishing gradient prevention.
- Strategy: **Transfer Learning** (Pre-trained on ImageNet).
- Output Layer: Custom 4-way Linear Classifier.

## Slide 5: Training Pipeline
- Loss Function: **CrossEntropyLoss**.
- Optimizer: **Adam**.
- Hyperparameters: Finetuned Learning Rate (LR), Batch Size (BS).
- Validation Accuracy Metrics: Over 94%.

## Slide 6: Vision Engineering (Preprocessing)
- Standardization: Resize (224x224).
- Normalization: Z-score scaling based on ImageNet stats.
- **Robust Augmentations**: Horizontal flips, Random rotations (10°), Color Jitter, and Affine shifts.

## Slide 7: Explainable AI (XAI)
- Technique: **Grad-CAM**.
- Mechanism: Gradient flow into the last Conv layer.
- Clinical Value: Visual verification of pathological regions (Heatmaps).

## Slide 8: Live Technical Demo
- Real-time Classification of various tumor types.
- Heatmap overlay visualization.
- Confidence score analysis per class.

## Slide 9: Future Technical Scope
- 3D UNet for segmentation.
- Transformer-based architectures (ViT).
- Mobile-inference (Quantization).

## Slide 10: Conclusion
- Empowering Radiologists with Deep Learning.
- Q&A Session focusing on Model Weights and Gradients.
